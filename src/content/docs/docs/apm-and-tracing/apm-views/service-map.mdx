The **Service Map** is a visual representation of the relationships and interdependencies between services in your workspace. It is built from your services' distributed traces and gives you a topology-level view of your architecture.

You can use it to understand service dependencies, traffic flow, and operational health at a glance.

## How the map is built

The map is built from distributed traces, so both sources of tracing feed it:

- **[eBPF monitoring](../../../kloudmate-agent/ebpf-observability/)** captures traces at the kernel level with no code change, so an eBPF-covered service appears on the map on its own. This works on VMs, hosts, and containers, and for [Kubernetes](../../../kloudmate-agent/auto-instrumentation/kubernetes/) workloads set to **eBPF**.
- **[Auto-instrumentation](../../../kloudmate-agent/auto-instrumentation/overview/)** and the OpenTelemetry SDK produce traces from inside the application.

Each service becomes a node under its `service.name`, and an edge is drawn wherever one service's trace calls another. A service needs a distinct `service.name` to appear as its own node. The agent gives an eBPF-monitored service the same name it carries once auto-instrumented, so it keeps one identity whether eBPF or the SDK is tracing it.

The map also draws calls where only one side is traced:

- **Calls into an eBPF-monitored service from a caller that sends no telemetry**, such as a script or an uninstrumented service. The caller is named after its Kubernetes workload, or shown as `incoming` when it can't be identified.
- **Calls to a database.** Databases don't send traces, so the calling service's trace is enough to draw the edge.

## Overview

Open **APM & Services** and select the **Service map** view. You can also open the map from a service's page: click **View in service map** in the **Service dependencies** panel.

![The Service map view of APM & Services, with the Refresh button, the Show external dependencies switch, the summary chips, and the Service Dependency Graph](./images/service-map-1.png)

Summary chips count the services, external dependencies, dependencies, services with errors, and erroring edges in the selected time range. A service or dependency counts as erroring when its error rate is 0.05% or higher.

The **Service map** view uses the same time range as the **List** view. Click **Refresh** to reload the map with the latest data, and turn on **Show external dependencies** to add the uninstrumented hosts your services call, such as third-party APIs, message queues, and managed databases outside your cluster. Databases running inside your cluster appear without the switch.

The main area displays the **Service Dependency Graph**. Each service is represented as a node showing its name, protocol, request rate, and average latency. The directed edges between nodes represent the call flow and dependencies between services.

Use the zoom controls on the bottom-right to zoom in and out, or click the expand icon to view the graph in full screen.

![The Service Dependency Graph in full screen, with Fit Graph, Clear Selection, the minimap, and the zoom controls](./images/service-map-2.png)

## Inspecting a Service

Click any service node on the graph to open the service details panel on the right.

![The Service details panel for frontend, with request count, request rate, average latency, error rate, inbound and outbound dependencies, and Open service page](./images/service-map-1.jpeg)

Click **Open service page** at the bottom of the panel to navigate to that service's APM dashboard, where you can inspect request trends, latency percentiles, traces, issues, alerts, and dashboards in more detail.

![The Service dependencies panel on the frontend service page, with tables of downstream dependencies, outbound calls, and upstream callers, and View in service map](./images/service-map-3.png)

## Inspecting a Dependency

Click any edge between two service nodes to open the dependency details panel on the right.

![The Dependency details panel for the edge from frontend-proxy to frontend, with source and target, request count, error rate, P99, P95, and P50 latency, and Open source and Open target](./images/service-map-2.jpeg)

Use **Open source** or **Open target** to navigate directly to the APM dashboard of either service involved in the dependency.

## Identifying Issues

Nodes and edges with errors are highlighted in red, making it easy to spot problematic services and dependencies at a glance. Click a highlighted node or edge to review its error rate and latency details.
