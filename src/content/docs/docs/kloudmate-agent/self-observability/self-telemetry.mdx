Collector self-telemetry is the deeper, performance-oriented view of the agent's embedded OpenTelemetry (OTel) Collector. Where the [health metrics](../health-metrics/) tell you whether the collector is up, self-telemetry tells you how well it is keeping up while it runs. It is **opt-in**, because it adds metric volume, and it is emitted **in-band** through the collector itself.

## What it reports

When enabled, the collector emits its standard `otelcol_*` metrics, including:

- **Exporter health:** queue size and capacity, send failures, and successfully sent items, so you can see whether the collector is falling behind on shipping data.
- **Receiver activity:** accepted and refused items, so you can see whether data is being dropped at intake.
- **Processor and process metrics:** internal pipeline and process counters.

These are the same metrics any OpenTelemetry Collector can produce, so they are familiar and well documented.

## Why it is opt-in and in-band

Two properties follow from where these metrics come from:

- **It adds cost.** The collector produces a metric series per component, which increases the metric volume you ingest. For that reason it is off by default, and you turn it on when you want the detail.
- **It dies with the collector.** Because these metrics flow through the collector, they stop when the collector stops. That is why they are not a liveness signal: liveness is the job of the out-of-band [health metrics](../health-metrics/).

The two tiers complement each other. If the health metrics show `kmagent.collector.up=1` but the collector self-telemetry goes silent, that divergence itself points to a wedged pipeline.

## Enabling it

Collector self-telemetry is turned on through the agent's settings. Once enabled, the `otelcol_*` metrics arrive in KloudMate alongside your other telemetry, and you can build dashboards and alerts on exporter queue depth and send failures.
